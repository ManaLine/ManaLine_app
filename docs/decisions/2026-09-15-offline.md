# Offline: what it means here

**Status: DRAFT AWAITING A DECISION.** Task 6 of
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-15-production-readiness.md`, which is a
stop-for-approval point. Nothing in Task 7 should be built until the five
questions below have answers.

Every recommendation here is a proposal with its reasoning shown, so it can be
disagreed with on the reasoning rather than on the conclusion.

---

## Why this is not a caching problem

`pubspec.yaml` lines 51–53 carry a commented-out `mana_line_offline_sync` path
dependency. Uncommenting it is not the task.

This app's money rules are enforced **server-side on purpose**. `record_collection`
is not a write — it is sixteen refusals wrapped around a write. Read out of
`pg_proc` on 2026-09-15:

**Refusals the device could check for itself**

- Collected amount must be positive
- Split amounts must be positive
- Payment splits must sum to the collected amount
- A guarantor id is required when the payer is a Guarantor
- Cannot record a payment for a future date
- Cannot record a payment before the loan was issued
- Add the payment to the receipt itself, not to one of its days

**Refusals that depend on state the device cannot see**

- Loan not found
- That receipt no longer exists
- That receipt belongs to a different loan
- That receipt is from a different collection window
- This customer is not the borrower on this loan
- Not authorized to record collections on this loan
- Collector is not a member of this business
- Collector must be the Owner or an Agent
- No BF assignment for this agent — the Owner must grant BF before collections can be credited

**The second list is the whole problem.** An offline collection is a write
that has not been checked yet, and seven of those nine can become true *while
the agent is offline* — the Owner deletes a loan, closes a day, reassigns an
area, removes the agent (which this app now warns about but permits), or the
collection window rolls over.

## What already exists in our favour

- **`idempotency_keys`** — `(idempotency_key, operation, business_id, person_id, response, created_at)`. It stores the **response**, so replaying a key returns the original answer rather than writing twice. A queue that retries is safe by construction. This is the single most important piece and it is already built.
- **`manaIdempotencyKey()`**, minted at entry construction and reused by every retry.
- **`flutter_secure_storage`**, already used by eight files for the session, the token, and the bulk wizard's resume point.
- **`sqflite`**, already a transitive dependency and **currently unused by `lib/`** — so a local queue needs no new package.

## The five questions

### 1. Which actions may be taken offline?

**Recommendation: recording a collection. Only that, at first.**

It is the one thing that genuinely happens in a village with no signal, and it
is most of daily use. Everything else — issuing a loan, granting BF, approving
a settlement, adding a member — is done sitting down, usually by the Owner,
usually where there is a signal. Each one also moves money in a way that needs
a server-side check *before* the person is told it worked.

The temptation will be "while we are here, let the whole app work offline".
That turns one queue with one refusal path into nine.

### 2. What does the agent see for a balance that may be stale?

**Recommendation: show it, and stamp it.** "As of 7:40 am" under any figure
read from the cache, and the stamp goes away the moment it is refreshed.

The alternative — hiding balances when offline — makes the app useless exactly
when it is needed. A stale balance is usually right; a hidden one is never
useful. But an unstamped stale balance is a *confidently wrong number on a
collection screen*, which CLAUDE.md names as worse than a crash because nobody
notices it.

**Open sub-question for you:** should a stale balance block *entering* a
collection, or only annotate it? I lean to annotate — the agent is collecting
what the customer hands over, not what the screen says.

### 3. What happens when a queued collection is REFUSED on sync?

**This is the hard one, and the reason this document exists.**

The agent has already told the customer the payment was received. They may
have written it in a paper book. The customer has gone. A refusal hours later
cannot un-tell them.

**Recommendation: a refused collection becomes an inbox item for the Owner,
never a silent drop, and never an automatic retry.**

Concretely:

- The queue entry moves to a `Refused` state carrying the server's own message — the sixteen above are written to be read by a person, and that is worth using.
- It appears in the Owner's notifications inbox as *"A collection Ramesh recorded on Tuesday could not be saved: …"*, with the amount, the customer, and the day.
- The **agent** sees it too, on their own screen, because they are the one who will have to go back.
- Nothing is retried automatically. A refusal is a fact about the world, not a transient error, and retrying it produces the same refusal at a time nobody is watching.

**What must not happen:** the queue silently dropping it, or the app "fixing"
it by re-pointing the payment at a different loan. Either turns a money error
into a money error nobody can find.

**Open sub-question for you:** should the Owner be able to *force* a refused
collection through from the inbox (accepting it as an adjustment), or must the
agent re-record it? The first is fewer steps and lets an Owner paper over a
real problem; the second is honest and slower.

### 4. Does an offline collection issue a receipt number?

**Recommendation: no server receipt number offline.**

Receipt numbers come from the server and must stay unique and sequential. A
device that invents one will collide, and two customers holding the same
receipt number is worse than neither holding one.

Offline, the app should show a **local reference** that is visibly not a
receipt — the idempotency key's short form, labelled as "not yet confirmed" —
and the real receipt number appears once it syncs.

**Open sub-question for you:** do your agents actually hand a customer
anything today, or is the paper book the record? If it is the paper book, this
question may not matter at all, and I would rather know than assume.

### 5. How long may a device stay offline before it refuses to keep accepting?

**Recommendation: a cap, and I suggest one working day.**

Unbounded queues are how twenty collections pile up behind a single refusal
that invalidated all of them — an agent removed from the business on Monday
morning keeps "collecting" all week.

At the cap the app stops accepting new collections and says why. It does not
delete anything already queued.

**Open sub-question for you:** one day, or one account period? One day is
safer and will occasionally annoy someone genuinely out of signal for two
days. You know how often that happens; I do not.

## What I would build first, once these are answered

A queue that holds **one** operation type, is keyed by the idempotency key that
already exists, retries only on transport failures, and routes every server
refusal to a person. Roughly: a `sqflite` table, a sync service, a state on the
collection screen, and an inbox notification type.

Sized honestly: **weeks, not days**, and most of it is question 3.

## What I am NOT recommending

- Offline-first for the whole app.
- Local recomputation of BF or `day_ledger`. Both are derived server-side from events, and a device recomputing them would produce a second answer that disagrees with the real one. Show the last known value with a timestamp.
- Any local write that bypasses an RPC. The RPCs are where the money rules live.
